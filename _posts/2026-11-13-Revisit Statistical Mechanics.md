# Lecture 1

At the very beginning, the professor define the thermodynamics. Thermodynamicsis aphenomenological description of equilibrium properties of macroscopic systems. Concept of equilibrium and how to describe (V, P) the system are introduced.  

An interesting point is that to describe the world, only knowing some mechanical principles, the force and work stuff, is not enough to describe phenomena like boiling a pot of water. We need something else.

Begin with two laws. 0th law is about the transactiong between equilibrium. Some rough proof is given, and indicates that equilibrium is characterized by a function of thermodynamic coordinates. The equilibrium means equal of something. The zero law further indicates the PV is constant along the isotherms in different equilibrium. One can use this to define tempeature (The ideal gas temperature scale).

Then, the first law is introduced. Beginning with adiabatically isolated system, the internal energy, is introduced. In general, $\mathrm{d} E=\mathrm{d}\kern{-4.3pt}\bar{\small\phantom{q}}\kern{-0.7pt} Q+ \mathrm{d}\kern{-4.3pt}\bar{\small\phantom{q}}\kern{-0.7pt} W$ and $\mathrm{d}\kern{-4.3pt}\bar{\small\phantom{q}}\kern{-0.7pt} W = P \mathrm{d}V$. $Q$ and $W$ are process related, while $E$ is the function of state. This can also be seen by the Joule’s Free Expansion Experiment.

The quasi-static transiformation is then also introduced. state function, generalized displacements, and the conjugate generalized forces. 

Response functiuons are introduced for a man to characterize the behavior of a system. Specifically, heat capacity is introduced as an example. The heat capacity in different situation and their relationships are considered.

# Lecture 2

Starting with $\mathrm{d} E=\mathrm{d}\kern{-4.3pt}\bar{\small\phantom{q}}\kern{-0.7pt} Q+ \mathrm{d}\kern{-4.3pt}\bar{\small\phantom{q}}\kern{-0.7pt} W$ and $\mathrm{d}\kern{-4.3pt}\bar{\small\phantom{q}}\kern{-0.7pt} W = P \mathrm{d}V$, we try to build somthing for $Q$ similar to $W$ and let $\mathrm{d}\kern{-4.3pt}\bar{\small\phantom{q}}\kern{-0.7pt} Q=$ something times $\mathrm{d}$ something. We suppose the former something to be temperature $T$, and we will find the latter one to be entropy.

Then the heat engine and refrigerator engine are included. why they are included. The reason, I think, first is the historic background, The first industrial revolution making the heat engine inportant. Second is because they are the two sides of one coin. Concept of efficience of a heat engine and the performance of a refrigerator are introduced.

Then, the second low of thermodynamics are introduced. Kelvin’s Statement (Heat engine) and Clausius’s Statement (Refrigerator). The equivalence of thses two statements are given. Proved by Contradiction.

Then, the carnot engine is given, which 3 conditions are needed to be satisfied. The circle is then included, The adiabatic process is discussed in detail where one can obtain that 

$$PV^\gamma = constant$$

Carnot's theorem that between two reservoirs (at temperatures $T_H$ and $T_C$) is more efficient than a Carnot engine operating between them is introduced.

Then, by introducing more Carnot engines in series and condiering their effecience relationships, the thermodynamic temperature scale is introduced. The relationship with the scale in Lect 1 is discussed.
